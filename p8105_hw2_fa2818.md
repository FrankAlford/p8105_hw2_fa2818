This dataset gives information about each subway station entrance and
exit in New York City. It has 1868 rows representing 1868 entrances or
exits and 19 columns representing variables consisting of subway line,
station name, station latitude and longitude, what subway routes pass
through, what kind of entrance it is (like stairs or elevator), if
entrance is allowed, if a ticket vending machine is available, and ADA
compliance. I imported the csv from my `data` folder and converted the
data types for route 8-11 to char from double, since the line names for
these columns were only numbers originally. I used clean_names to make
all variable names in lower snake case, used `select` to pick the
variables of interest, and `mutate` to convert the values of the `entry`
variable from chars “Yes” and “No” into logical values of TRUE and
FALSE. The data is not tidy since the route variables are spread across
11 different variables, most of which are empty.

The Trash Wheel dataset has 1269 rows combined from Mr. Trash Wheel,
Professor Trash Wheel, and Gwynda. Each row represents an instance where
a dumpster’s trash was collected. Variables include the date the trash
was collected, the weight of collected trash measured in tons, volume
measured in cubic yards, and frequency for different kinds of trash like
plastic bottles and cigarette butts. I imported each excel file,
filtered out na rows including an entry in Professor Trash Wheel’s
plastic bottle column with value “dive”. I changed the variable names to
lower snake case, removed rows without dumpster information, rounded
sports ball to integer, and added a column specifying which dataset it
came from using `mutate`. I then combined the datasets into
`trash_wheel_df` to calculate that Professor Trash Wheel collected
294.96 tons of trash total, and Gwynda collected 18120 cigarette butts
in June of 2022.

I imported the baseline dataset with `skip=1` since the first row
contained descriptions of the variables, not the names. I then used

before their baseline age, and kept participants with missing MCI onset
age since this means they did not develop MCI. The baseline dataset
includes participant ID, age at baseline, sex, years of education, APOE4
carrier status, and age at MCI onset.
